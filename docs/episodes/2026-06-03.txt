I think there's a real opportunity here and a real risk. Let's move from security to the broader pushback, because there's a real theme emerging right now of regulators and professionals pumping the brakes on how all this technology is being built.

The biggest of these is about AI search. Both TechCrunch and Engadget reported that Google will now let website publishers opt out of having their content used in generative AI search features, the AI-written summaries that appear at the top of results and answer your question without you ever clicking through to the original site. This is happening because of regulation in the United Kingdom; UK regulators are requiring Google to offer this opt-out tool. It'll be tested in the UK first and then rolled out globally. And here's the crucial detail that makes it actually meaningful: Google says opting out of AI search won't hurt your placement in regular search results.

Why does that matter so much? Because until now, publishers have been stuck in an impossible bind. AI summaries scrape your work and answer the reader's question right there, so the reader never visits your site, never sees your ads, never subscribes. But if you tried to block the AI, you risked vanishing from regular search too, which for most publishers is a death sentence. By decoupling the two, regulators are giving websites a real choice for the first time. This is one of those stories that sounds dry but has enormous stakes for the open web. The entire economic model of online journalism and independent publishing depends on people actually clicking through, and AI summaries have been quietly strangling that. Whether enough publishers opt out to change Google's behavior remains to be seen, but at least the door is now open.

This is a pattern worth watching across many fields.
