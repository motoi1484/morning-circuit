Researchers have discovered a new way that websites can spy on the people who visit them — and this one is sneaky. It turns out that by analyzing the activity of your solid-state drive — that's the SSD, the storage where all your files and data live on a modern computer — a website can learn things about you. And the part that should make you sit up is that they can do this using simple JavaScript, which is the basic code that runs on nearly every website you visit. There's nothing exotic going on here. Now, why is this concerning? Because this is what's known as a side-channel attack. The website isn't breaking into your computer in the traditional sense, and it isn't asking your permission. It's just quietly observing a kind of physical signature — the behavior of your hardware — and inferring information from it. It's the digital equivalent of figuring out what's happening inside a house by listening to the pipes. This is the sort of cat-and-mouse game that plays out constantly in the security world: researchers find a clever new way to extract information, and then browser makers and hardware manufacturers scramble to close the door. I'm flagging this not to scare you, but because it's a good reminder that privacy online is an ongoing battle being fought at levels most of us never even think about.
